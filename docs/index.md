# How to play FULL THROTTLE

You are Agent Nova Kane in an over-the-top spy movie, driving the armed KESTREL-88 (the red Countach-style wedge with the huge wing) along the coast road of Bayshore, a sunny 80s beach town, and on into the chemical plant and the Nevada desert. There are three levels. Pick one on the title screen with the left and right arrows (or tap the level bar), then press Enter:

- **Level 1: Bayshore, THE HIGHWAY ASSASSINATION.** Sixteen scenes along the coast road, about five to seven minutes to play. Survive the Syndicate's hit squad and take down **THE JUGGERNAUT** at the end of the road.
- **Level 2: the Chemical Plant, THE FORMULA.** Eight scenes through the Volta Chemical Works at night: twisty S-bends, banked helix ramps, loops and glass **pipes** you drive all the way round inside. Take down **THE ALCHEMIST**'s tesla tanker at the end, and dodge his lightning (see *The bosses* below).
- **Level 3: the Nevada Desert, ALL IN** (new in 1.5). Nine scenes down Highway 93 at sunset, with the neon of Las Vegas lit up dead ahead on the horizon, getting bigger as you go: The State Line, The Dry Wash, Snake Eyes, Joshua Flats, Red Rock Pass, Last Chance Gas, Jackpot Junction, The City Lights and The Finale. Fight off the **SNAKE EYES** biker gang and take down **THE BIG DEALER**'s motorcycle carrier at the end (see *Level 3* below).

Wrap a level and Enter on the rating screen takes you straight on to the next one (R reshoots the same level instead). On the title, the number keys 1, 2 and 3 pick a level too.

The camera rides just behind and above your car: the highway runs away to the horizon, and traffic and the Syndicate come up out of the distance.

## The track

The road is a stunt track, and the camera goes everywhere it goes:

- **Bends and humps.** Twisting bends, crests and a run of humps on a raised road. A flat bend pulls the car toward its outside edge, so steer into it to hold your lane.
- **Banked corners.** Most bends lean into the turn, which takes most of that pull away. Two are steep **banked walls**: stay up on the high side for a moment for a **WALL RIDE**.
- **Gap jumps.** Twice the road climbs to a yellow-striped lip and just stops. You fly the gap whatever your speed, and nothing can hit you while you're in the air. Hit the lip on turbo for **TURBO AIR**: a higher arc and more points. Land on an enemy to crush it.
- **Full loops.** Two in the chase and more in the finale. Go round for **LOOP THE LOOP**, or keep turbo on for **TURBO LOOP**.
- **The corkscrew.** The road rolls over once, up high. Get through it for **CORKSCREW**.
- **Pipes (level 2).** The road runs into a glass tube and rolls right round it, so you drive up the wall, along the ceiling and back down. Some pipes go round twice, and the big one in The Toxic Run goes round three times. Get through for **PIPE RUN**.
- **Upside down.** A kill at the top of a loop, in the middle of the corkscrew or on the ceiling of a pipe scores an **UPSIDE-DOWN KILL**.
- Yellow-and-black stripes on the shoulders mean a loop, gap or corkscrew is just ahead. The director warns you a few seconds out ("LOOP ahead! Keep your foot down!", "The road ENDS up there! Hit the lip fast and FLY!"), calls it as you go ("UPSIDE DOWN!", "FLY!") and reacts when you come out ("A full LOOP!", "And DOWN! Stick it!").

## Driving

- **Steering** eases in over a tenth of a second, so a tap nudges you across and a hold takes a whole lane. Steering back the other way bites harder, so you can flick out of trouble.
- **Drift.** Hold **Shift** (left little finger; **A** or **RB** on a gamepad; the big **DRIFT** button on a phone) and steer, kart-style, in either order: press it and then steer, or steer and then press it, and the KESTREL kicks its tail out and slides. A press is remembered for a quarter of a second, and holding it and then turning the wheel starts a slide too, so a steer that comes a beat late still catches. It only needs a touch of steer, and it works when you're braking. While you slide, steer into the drift to tighten it or away to swing wide, and a drift into a bend carves right through its pull. Holding the slide charges a boost: the sparks off the rear wheels turn **blue**, then **orange**, then **pink**. Let go to fire it: a short burst of speed with flames in the charge colour, plus a little turbo back. An orange release scores **POWER DRIFT**, a pink one **ULTRA DRIFT**. A slide carries on over bumps and jumps (it doesn't charge in the air), and a hit knocks the charge back about one colour instead of ending the slide.
- **Turbo** (Space, under your left thumb) kicks in with a shove of speed the moment it lights, the camera's lens widens and the air streams past. Like a drift boost, it counts for turbo rams, turbo jumps and turbo loops.
- **The camera** leans a little into turns and drifts, swings out to show the car's slide, and pulls wider as you go faster. The car leans on its springs and squashes when it lands.

## About the look

Version 1.0 is the cel-shaded art pass, with 80s beach vibes. Everything has flat bands of light and ink outlines. On the left of the road is the beach with umbrellas and lifeguard towers. On the right are the promenade and pastel art-deco hotels. The bay, the skyline, a pier Ferris wheel and a lighthouse sit on the horizon.

The KESTREL-88, the Syndicate cars, the traffic and every building, palm and prop were modelled and rendered in Unreal Engine 5. The road, sky and sea are drawn by the game in the same style (since 1.4 the explosions and fire are rendered in Unreal too).

Version 1.1 makes level 1 much longer (seven new scenes: The Pier, Neon Mile, The Marina, The Cliff Road, The Lighthouse, The Last Mile and The Causeway, plus Sunset Strip before the finale), keeps the palms, lamps and hotels off the road (none stand where a loop comes back down or a bend swings past any more), and gives every car its tyres on both sides.

Version 1.2 lights the whole thing. The road is lit by the sun (by day) or the moon (at night) according to which way it faces, so banked corners and loops shade as they turn, and it fades into haze in the distance. At night the plant's floodlights throw sodium-orange pools across the lanes, the tube lamps light the inside of the pipes cyan, and your headlights light the road ahead. Explosions, missiles and lightning light up the road around them. Tail lights, brake lights, the KESTREL's pink underglow, tracers, muzzle flashes, fire, sparks and embers all glow, and the frame gets a soft bloom, a colour grade, a vignette and a little film grain. At speed the picture zooms out from the middle and speed lines stream past. The car's paint catches a moving sheen and a rim of the nearest light. Explosions leave scorch marks, drifts leave skid marks and tyre smoke, and smoke is cel-shaded.

New detail on the first two levels (1.2): overhead sign gantries (green highway signs and neon in Bayshore; hazard-striped pipe bridges with blinking beacons in the plant), sailboats on the bay, gulls, a Bayshore blimp, sun rays, a glitter path on the water and a lens flare off the sun; and in the plant, a storm with lightning in it, searchlights sweeping the sky, steam off the stacks, red beacons on the towers, glowing vats, cat's eyes on the lane lines and drifting chemical motes. All of this is drawn by the game in the same cel style; the Unreal sprites are unchanged.

**Level 2, the Volta Chemical Works**, is our own night-shift chemical plant: deep blue steel decks with hazard-yellow kerbs and sodium-orange rails, teal storage tanks, magenta gas spheres, striped smokestacks, distillation towers, pipework and vats of glowing violet chemical, a violet chemical lake and the far side of the works under a big striped moon. It was modelled and rendered in Unreal Engine 5 like level 1.

## Level 3: the Nevada desert (1.5)

The open road to Las Vegas at dusk: a synthwave sunset with the sun going down behind the ridges, the first stars coming out, and the city lit up at the end of the highway with a beam off its pyramid and searchlights sweeping the sky. The road is mostly long straights and rolling dips, but the movie crew still built a few set pieces out here: gaps over dry washes (the bridge is out), two loops, a corkscrew through the canyon and banked walls round the red rock. Along the way are Joshua trees, barrel cacti, red sandstone, buttes, telephone poles with sodium lamps, a neon motel, a lonely gas stop, casino pylons with giant dice on top, green highway signs and tumbleweeds bouncing across the lanes.

**The SNAKE EYES** are an outlaw biker gang on choppers: long forks, ape-hanger bars, black leather cuts with a pair of dice on the back. A biker rides up beside one of your doors and starts whirling a chain over his head (it glows red and the director shouts **CHAIN!**). Get a car's width away before he swings it, or steer hard into him first: a chopper can't take a side hit from the KESTREL, and slamming one off his bike scores **BIKER SLAM**. You can shoot them while they're ahead of you, too.

**THE BIG DEALER** rides at the end of the highway in a black-and-chrome motorcycle carrier, a two-deck trailer loaded with choppers and its tail ramp down. It never stops sending bikers: one rolls off the ramp every couple of seconds. Below two thirds of its armor, bikers also launch off the kicker on the top deck and fly at you (a pink mark on the road shows where they'll land, so steer away), and it brake-checks you: its tail lights flare and blink, then it drops back right onto your bonnet. Below a third it empties the whole truck. Shoot the carrier (missiles help), slam the bikers, and don't take too long, or he gets away.

## The highlight reel (1.3)

Wrap a level and, before the rating, the movie plays you its own highlights: the best five moments of your run, in the order they happened, then the boss going down twice from two angles. It's about forty seconds, in scope letterbox with film titles. Each clip gets its own camera (a low **bumper** camera, a **tracking** shot alongside, a **crane** that swoops down, an **aerial**, a **dolly** that slides across, a **kill cam** that swings onto what's exploding, or the chase camera) and slows right down on the money shot. Only takes that made it are used: a scene you wrecked and reshot only shows the take that worked. Press Enter (or click, or tap) to skip to the rating.

## Explosions and damage (1.3)

Explosions are mostly fire now: a white-hot flash, a fireball that swells and climbs and burns from yellow through orange to deep red, burning chunks and body panels flying out, a fountain of sparks, embers drifting up for seconds and a patch left burning on the road, with only a little smoke at the end. Each one lights up the road, the cars and everything near it, and big ones flash the whole screen.

The KESTREL shows what it's been through. Below about 70% armor it's dented and scraped (a mirror gone, a lamp smashed, the aerial bent); below 40% it's trashed (half the top wing torn off and drooping, the bumper hanging and dragging sparks along the road, the blower knocked askew, a missile pod hanging loose). Soot builds up, the blower steams, then smokes black, then burns. Every so often a piece comes off and bounces down the road behind you. A wrench puts it back together. Damaged Syndicate cars smoke, spark and burn too, and missiles tear panels off them.

## Cars in motion and real fire (1.4)

The KESTREL is no longer one rigid picture. It's put together on the fly from parts modelled and rendered in Unreal: the body, and four wheels that turn with your speed (you can see the tread blocks roll and the gold rims spin). The front wheels steer with you, and countersteer when you drift. The body rides on a light suspension: it bobs over the road's bumps and seams, squats when you floor it, dips when you brake, leans out of corners and drifts, slams down and rebounds when you land a jump, and jolts when you're hit, scrape the rail or a henchman grabs on. The mud flaps are gone so you can see the rear tyres. The Syndicate rammers and gunship SUVs are built the same way: their wheels spin, they lean as they swerve and kick when you hit them, and a rammer shudders while it revs up to charge. All three damage stages still show, and a trashed KESTREL runs on a wobbly rear wheel.

Explosions are real fire now, not stacks of circles. The fireballs, the burst of flame tongues at the first instant, the licking flames of anything burning and the charred debris were all made in Unreal and are played back as animated frames: the fireball boils out in billowing, irregular lumps, climbs, burns from white-hot through yellow, orange and red, and breaks up into soot. Every blast layers several of them, each a different variant, size, turn and timing, on top of the flash, the light on the road, the sparks and the embers, so no two look alike.

## The director

The whole demo is shot like a film set. The director talks to you the whole time, in the bar under the film strip at the top of the screen:

- **Scenes.** Level 1 is sixteen scenes: The Getaway, The Tail, The Ambush, The Jump, The Convoy, Crossfire, The Wolf Pack, The Pier, Neon Mile, The Marina, The Cliff Road, The Lighthouse, The Last Mile, The Causeway, Sunset Strip and The Finale. Level 2 is eight: The Factory Gate, The Tubes, Spin Cycle, The Reactor, Pipe Dreams, Mega Vat, The Toxic Run and The Finale. Level 3 is nine, from The State Line to The Finale. Each opens with a clapper slate (scene and take), the director's setup for it ("Car carrier, then the road just ENDS! Turbo and fly the gap!") and a shouted **ACTION!**
- **He reacts to you.** A chopper coming in, a henchman landing on the car ("He's on the car! Slam him into the wall!"), a wall smash, a kill, a near miss, a ramp jump, a loop, a gap jump, the corkscrew, a banked wall, a scraped rail, a civilian hit, taking fire, low armor and pickups each get a line. When you stop shooting with enemies ahead he says "Take him out, now!". When the road goes quiet he wants near misses and sparks off the barrier. A rammer blinking pale (about to charge) gets you a warning.
- **CUT, again.** Wreck the star car and he yells **CUT!**. A clapper for the next take comes up and you reshoot that scene from the top, with the box office as it stood when the scene began and fresh armor on the car. You have three reels of film for retakes (FILM LEFT, top right). Wreck it a fourth time and the run ends: "We're out of film", rated B-Movie.

## Controls

Since 1.7.1 the keyboard splits the work between your hands. The **left hand drives**: WASD steers, the little finger holds **Shift** to drift and the thumb hits **Space** for turbo. The **right hand shoots**: **J** fires the guns and **K** a missile, or use the mouse (hold the left button for the guns, right-click for a missile). Drifting needs only Shift and a steering key, and turbo is the thumb on its own, so no move needs two left-hand keys and the thumb at once.

Rather steer with the arrow keys? That's the other layout: your right hand steers on the arrows and the left hand, now free, fires with **Z** (guns) and **X** (missile). Shift and Space still drift and turbo. Z and X only fire in the arrows layout, so a stray left-hand finger can't fire them while you drive with WASD (and J and K don't fire in the arrows layout). The game switches layout by itself as soon as you steer with the other set of keys in a race, and remembers it.

| WASD layout | Arrows layout | Gamepad | Phone | Does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WASD | Arrow keys | Left stick or d-pad | Drag on the left half | Steer; up speeds up, down brakes |
| Shift (hold, and steer) | Shift (hold, and steer) | A or RB (hold) | DRIFT (hold) | Drift; let go to fire the drift boost |
| Space | Space | LB or RT | TURBO | Speed burst; turbo rams hurt enemies and launch you further off ramps |
| J, or hold the left mouse button | Z, or hold the left mouse button | X | FIRE | Twin machine guns |
| K, or right-click (one per press) | X, or right-click (one per press) | B | MSL | Homing missile (pick up orange **M** crates for more) |

| Keyboard | Gamepad | Phone | Does |
|---|---|---|---|
| P or Esc | Start | II | Pause |
| C (on the title or pause screen) | Y | CONTROLS button | The controls screen |

Enter or Space moves the menus on (A or Start on a gamepad). On the title, the left and right arrows (or the d-pad) pick the level.

### The controls screen (1.7)

Press **C** on the title or the pause screen (or click **CONTROLS** there, or press **Y** on a gamepad) to see every control. Each action has one key in the **WASD** column and one in the **ARROWS** column (the layout you're using is marked **IN USE**). Pick a slot with the arrow keys and press Enter, or click it, then press the new key; Delete clears a slot, and **RESET TO DEFAULTS** puts everything back. Within a layout a key can only do one job, so giving it to a new action takes it off the old one. Keys you set in 1.7.0 go back to the new defaults once, because the layouts changed. P, M, Esc and Enter keep their own jobs and can't be given away. The mouse buttons, the gamepad and the phone buttons are fixed. Your keys are saved on this computer (inside WispOS, in the app's own storage) and come back next time.

### Gamepad (1.7)

Any standard gamepad (Xbox, PlayStation and most others) works as soon as you press a button on it: left stick or d-pad to steer (push the stick up or down, or the d-pad, to speed up or brake), **A** or **RB** to drift, **X** for the guns, **B** for a missile, **LB** or **RT** for turbo, **Start** to pause. In the menus **A** or **Start** goes on, the stick or d-pad picks, **Y** opens the controls and **B** backs out. The screens switch their prompts to the pad's buttons when you use it. Unplugging the pad mid-race pauses the game.

## The cast

- **Biker**: weaves across the lanes and takes potshots.
- **Rammer** (purple muscle car with a ram bar): lines up behind you, blinks pale, then charges. Dodge it.
- **Gunship SUV**: sits ahead and fires bursts. Drops a missile crate.
- **Armored truck**: soaks up hits and drops explosive barrels (green chemical drums in the plant). Use missiles. Drops repairs.
- **Syndicate chopper** (new in 1.3): a purple helicopter that comes in low over the road, crabs alongside and lowers henchmen on a rope, one at a time, aiming for your car. A pink target on the road shows where each one will land, and it follows you: swerve hard and he lands on the tarmac instead (**DODGED THE DROP**). Guns and missiles can bring the chopper down (it's tough, so missiles help) for a big burning crash, a missile crate and a repair. The men on the rope are too high for the guns.
- **Henchmen on the car.** One that lands grabs a door (left or right, two at most), drags the car toward his side and punches through the window now and then, costing armor. Get rid of him by:
  - **slamming his side into the wall**: steer hard into the barrier on his side for a **WALL SMASH**;
  - **sideswiping** another car with his side (**SIDESWIPE**);
  - or **going upside down**, at the top of a loop, through the corkscrew or on a pipe's ceiling, and he falls off (**GRAVITY CHECK**).
- **The bosses.** Each gets away if you take too long (about 70 seconds).
  - **THE JUGGERNAUT** (level 1) fires spreads of bullets, drops explosive barrels and calls in bikers, and then a chopper, as it weakens.
  - **THE ALCHEMIST** (level 2) fights with electricity from the two tesla coils on his tanker, on a long open stretch of road after the last scene. Everything he does is warned first:
    - **Lane arc.** A lane turns yellow with cyan edges, stripes march down it and the coils crackle toward it. About a second later a bolt runs the length of the lane. Change lanes.
    - **Lock-on.** Reticles chase you across the road, turn red when they lock, and lightning falls on them from the storm. Keep moving.
    - **Shock wave.** The tanker discharges a crackling fence that rolls down the road at you. One lane is its dead gap, lit green all the way to you between two green posts. Be in the gap (or in the air) when it passes, for **DODGED! +200**.
  - Below two thirds of his health he overcharges (two lanes at once, faster warnings). Below a third it's a meltdown: shock waves back to back, three lock-ons, double arcs. Damage costs you armor as usual.
- **Civilians**: dodge them. Wrecking one costs 300.
- **Car carrier**: drive up its ramp for a jump (with turbo for a bigger one).

## Scoring and the rating

Destruction, near misses (passing within a hair of a car) and stunts (ramp jumps, gap jumps, loops, the corkscrew, wall rides, upside-down kills, airborne kills, turbo rams, double kills, threading between two cars) earn points, multiplied by your combo. Armor left at the wrap is a bonus. At the end the director rates the take: **B-Movie**, **Action Hero**, **Blockbuster** or **Legendary Director**. Running out of film (a fourth wreck) caps the rating at B-Movie; letting the boss escape caps it at Action Hero. Each level keeps its own best take.

## Music

The title screen plays "Bit-Beast Dash" on a loop while you pick your level, starting again from the top each time you come back to the title. If your browser won't play sound until you've touched the game, it starts with your first key or click. It fades out as the clapper comes down on a race.

All three levels play "Pixel Adventure", the game's own song, on a loop from the first ACTION! to the wrap. It sits under the sound effects and dips for a moment each time the director puts up a line, a scene opens or something big explodes. Pausing pauses the song; a retake carries on from where it stopped, and a new run starts it from the top. Press **M** to turn all sound off, music included; the game remembers it next time.

## Smooth running (1.7.2)

The game runs its physics at a steady 120 steps a second, whatever your screen does, and since 1.7.2 it draws every frame in between the last two steps. On a 120 or 144 Hz screen every frame now shows the car move (before, about one frame in six stood still, which read as a judder), and the race plays at exactly the same speed at 60, 120 or 144 Hz. Smoke, fire, sparks and tyre smoke come out at the same rate on any screen too.

Every picture the level uses is decoded and handed to the graphics card while the title and the scene card are up, so nothing has to be loaded in the middle of a race: the first explosion, the first chopper and the boss rolling in no longer stall a frame. The game also makes much less throwaway work while you drive, so the browser's memory clean-ups are small and rare. Nothing about the look or the handling has changed.
