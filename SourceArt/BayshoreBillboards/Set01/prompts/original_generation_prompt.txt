Create a concept-art sheet of NINE satirical roadside billboards for FULL THROTTLE, an over-the-top 1980s driving-action game. These advertisements belong in BAYSHORE, a fictional coastal resort town with pastel Art Deco hotels, palms, waterfront highways, a marina, a Ferris wheel, and pink sunset clouds.

Bayshore sells paradise to tourists while its lawyers, casinos, and restaurants quietly fight over their remaining money.

PRESENTATION
Arrange nine distinct billboard designs in a clean 3-by-3 grid. Each billboard is a horizontal 2:1 rectangle, shown straight-on with its entire advertising face visible. Narrow neutral gutters. Prioritize the actual advertisement artwork, without surrounding street scenes or support structures.

ART DIRECTION
Authentic, slightly tacky 1980s regional advertising: airbrushed portraits, hand-painted mascots, exaggerated food photography translated into illustration, bold condensed lettering, cheesy luxury crests, cheap starbursts, and aggressively confident salesmanship.

Use restrained cel shading, crisp silhouettes, selective dark outlines, and enough painterly detail to sit comfortably in a stylized game environment. Mix coral, mint, turquoise, cream, tomato red, deep navy, and fake metallic gold. Give each business its own recognizable identity.

Light sun bleaching, salt stains, and print wear at the edges. Keep headlines pristine and readable. These businesses are still operating and enthusiastically ripping people off.

COMEDY DIRECTION
Play every advertisement completely straight. The businesses believe these are excellent ads. The humor comes from greed, brazen admissions, sexual innuendo, and visual contradictions. Characters should look professionally pleased with themselves.

Every billboard needs one immediate visual gag and one huge headline readable during a high-speed chase. Small secondary copy is a bonus for paused screenshots. Reproduce the supplied wording exactly.

THE NINE DESIGNS

1. LEN FENDER — INJURY ATTORNEY
Main headline: “STAY DOWN. I’M ON MY WAY.”
Secondary: “Before the ambulance, if traffic’s good.”
Visual: A magnificently sleazy lawyer in a cream suit and gold watch, sprinting with an open briefcase. Loose contracts fly behind him. His enormous, reassuring advertising smile remains perfectly composed. Emergency yellow, navy, and red; the urgency of a furniture liquidation sale.

2. DICK SETTLE — PERSONAL INJURY LAW
Main headline: “YOUR PAIN. MY BOAT.”
Secondary: “Let’s make this a big settlement.”
Visual: An immaculate lawyer posing proudly on a ridiculous yacht, one polished loafer resting on a giant neck brace like a hunting trophy. Expensive marina-ad composition, champagne gold and ocean teal. His portrait occupies almost half the billboard.

3. REAR-END RICK — ACCIDENT LAW
Main headline: “READING THIS WHEN YOU CRASHED?”
Secondary: “Let’s sue the billboard.”
Visual: Rick grins and points directly at his own enormous headline. A small illustrated car has crumpled against the edge of the advertisement, bending its decorative border. Loud red, white, and blue. Make the self-incriminating confidence the entire joke.

4. THE LOOSE PELICAN — CASINO CRUISES
Main headline: “BLOW YOUR SETTLEMENT HERE.”
Secondary: “Neck braces drink free.”
Visual: A sunburned tourist in a neck brace triumphantly holds a cocktail while a pelican dealer discreetly rakes away an enormous mountain of his chips. Glittering offshore casino luxury. Magenta, turquoise, and counterfeit gold. Make the tourist look convinced he is winning.

5. SECOND MORTGAGE CASINO
Main headline: “COLLEGE ISN’T FOR EVERYONE.”
Secondary: “Bet on yourself.”
Visual: A smug tuxedoed mascot feeds a graduation-cap-shaped piggy bank into a slot machine. The machine’s three jackpot symbols are matching suburban houses. Aspirational family-finance advertising corrupted into a casino promotion. Burgundy, cream, gold, and respectable serif lettering.

6. THE SINKING FUND — CASINO & RESORT
Main headline: “THE ATM BELIEVES IN YOU.”
Secondary: “Another withdrawal. Another chance.”
Visual: An airbrushed ATM with an impossibly reassuring smile places one comforting white-gloved hand on a gambler’s shoulder while extending its other hand for his wallet. Present this as a luxurious hospitality amenity. Midnight blue, soft gold, intimate spotlighting.

7. CAPTAIN SHUCK’S — OYSTER BAR
Main headline: “WE SHUCK. YOU SUCK.”
Secondary: “Bring a date. Lower your standards.”
Visual: A deeply self-satisfied oyster mascot in a captain’s hat lounges on crushed ice beside lemon wedges. Ridiculous eyebrows, little gold chain, seafood-platter glamour. Weathered nautical cream, sea green, and bright red lettering. Suggestive through attitude and wording.

8. BURN NOTICE — HOT WINGS
Main headline: “YOUR ASS HAS A SEQUEL.”
Secondary: “Part II starts tomorrow.”
Visual: A chicken dressed as an excessively serious 1980s action hero walks away from a tiny exploding porcelain toilet, carrying a basket of wings. Compose it like an airbrushed action-movie VHS cover converted into a restaurant ad. Flame orange, black, yellow, huge dramatic typography.

9. BIG DICK’S — FOOTLONG FRANKS
Main headline: “FINALLY. TWELVE HONEST INCHES.”
Secondary: “Measured before grilling.”
Visual: A proud mustached cook with an open-collar shirt and thick gold chain presents a spectacularly ordinary hot dog beside a conspicuously oversized ruler. The ruler is obviously dishonest: its markings start at four. Cheerful red-and-mustard roadside food advertising, with absurdly sincere product presentation.

FINAL REQUIREMENTS
Give each design a different layout and graphic personality. The headline must dominate; supporting copy must remain secondary. Keep visual jokes simple enough to understand at thumbnail size. Use the exact business names and copy. All businesses are fictional.

The finished sheet should feel like evidence that Bayshore’s entire economy consists of getting injured, winning a settlement, and immediately spending it somewhere worse.
